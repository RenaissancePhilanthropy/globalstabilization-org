Summit survey insights

Summary

  1. Names for the field vary

    About half of those who named the field used some form of “stabilization”: unmodified, Earth or planetary, or climate stabilization, in roughly equal numbers. “Interventions” and a range of other descriptions make up most of the rest. Some respondents questioned the term, saying stability “isn't reality” for many people or that it can read as softening SAI.

  2. Term use differs by time in the field

    Respondents in the field under a year used a form of stabilization more often than those with five or more years, who more often used terms such as Earth system interventions, climate engineering, restoration or repair. The groups are small, so treat the difference as indicative.

  3. Many respondents are new to the field

    Nearly two thirds have been involved for under five years, and about one in five for under a year. Newer respondents were more likely to be under 45.

  4. Broad agreement on core approaches

    Nearly everyone included stratospheric aerosols, sea ice thickening, marine cloud brightening and slowing glaciers. Fewer included mitigation and adaptation measures, with fossil fuel phase-out and sea walls lowest. A majority included early warning for tipping points. Newer respondents included cool roofs, reforestation, coral breeding and early warning more often than those five or more years in.

  5. Gaps named: support and a path to testing

    Asked for the field's biggest gap, respondents most often named public and political support and a credible path to testing and engineering. Governance comes next, then defining the field’s scope, research on impacts, funding and narrowing the options.

  6. What respondents asked of ARC/CEF

    Connecting projects with funders and a shared map of interventions and who works on them tied on average rank; the map drew one more first-place vote. Joining a working group was the most selected way to stay involved, and many offered to make the case to policymakers.

  7. Feedback on the summit

    Hallway conversations, specific sessions and the opening were valued most. The most common suggestions: fewer parallel sessions, more open debate including critics, tighter panels, more unstructured time, a clearer account of what the field covers beyond SAI, and changes to the Day 1 tracks. About half said it should happen every year. Policymakers, critics and Global South voices were named most often as missing.

Signal strength. Directional. Self-selected attendees, more than half of them US nationals, surveyed days after a summit organized around stabilization, by the summit's organizers. The free-text themes are our coding of what people wrote.

Gaps and requests

Gaps respondents named

  1. Public and political support

    The gap named most often.

  2. A credible path to testing

    Named second: staged outdoor trials, engineering timelines and conditions for any future decision to deploy.

  3. Governance

    Domestic and international frameworks, accountability and liability, and preparing governments.

  4. Narrowing the options

    Faster techno-economic assessment to tell viable interventions apart.

  5. Funding

    Funding beyond a few donors, and for long-term research.

  6. The field's scope

    Naming sub-fields, such as ice sheets and warming feedbacks, so regional work isn't tied to debates over SRM.

What respondents asked of ARC/CEF

  1. Connecting projects with funders

    Tied for first on average rank; coordination among funders also came up.

  2. A shared map

    Interventions and who works on them. Tied for first on average rank, with one more first-place vote.

  3. Working groups

    Joining one was the most selected way to stay involved, and a good share offered to lead or co-lead one.

  4. A wider room

    Policymakers, critics and Global South voices were named most often as missing.

  5. The next summit

    About half said every year. Common requests: fewer parallel tracks and more room for debate.

  6. Clarity on scope

    Several asked for a clearer account of what the field covers beyond SAI.

About this survey. Attendees of the Global Risk and Stabilization Summit (Reykjavík, 12–13 October 2026) were invited to a short survey afterwards. Responses are self-selected and more than half of them US nationals, so read the figures as directional. Counts include partial responses wherever the question was answered. Free-text answers were grouped into themes by us; quotes are unattributed. The summit ran under the Chatham House Rule.
