About the summit
Convening leaders across domains who are working to better predict, prepare for, and prevent catastrophic climate risks that threaten to drive large-scale disruption.

ARC, supported by numerous partners and sponsors, will host the summit on October 12–13, 2026, in Reykjavík, Iceland. The range of participants will include scientists, philanthropic funders, entrepreneurs, policymakers, Indigenous leaders, youth networks, and Global South organizations, all of whom are actors who rarely share the same room but whose engagement is critical for legitimacy. The current moment is an important inflection point for the field, and this Summit exists to set it up for success.

Objectives

CEF supports events, roadmaps, and synthesis reports, all aimed at facilitating difficult conversations, not flattening them.

  • Synthesize knowledge on catastrophic climate risks
  • Identify decision-relevant pathways
  • Surface open questions and key debates
  • Build cross-field visibility and enduring collaborations

Day One

A comprehensive view of emerging nonlinear climate risks with specific climate system deep dives.

Day Two

Spotlighting challenges across technologies, response options, governance funding, industry, communication, and more, and how these will shape the pathway to future preparedness.

Field Synthesis Report

The report offers a high-resolution synthesis of efforts to better predict, prepare for, and prevent catastrophic climate risks, across four pillars and a synthesis.

1Sensemaking
2Preparedness & Prevention
3Governance
4Legitimacy & License
5Synthesis

The synthesis report is an independent document written by the roughly 40 authors that are contributing. It is not a summary of the summit, and we will not run a consensus or sign-on process with attendees.

Attending the summit does not imply that you endorse the report, any particular intervention, or the stabilization framing. Attending means you are taking part in the conversation and nothing more.
