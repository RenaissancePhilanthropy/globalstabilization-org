Adjusted for response bias

People using rating scales lean toward some answers regardless of the question: agreeing, rating everything important, or sticking to the middle or the extremes (Baumgartner & Steenkamp 2001). This room put nearly every sticker on the important side of every board. This view re-expresses each vote relative to how that role voted across all nine boards: it subtracts the role's average and divides by its spread, a group-level form of the score standardization reviewed by Fischer (2004). The crossing now means "this role's typical rating," and each axis end is three standard deviations from it.

Stickers carry no names, so the adjustment works at the level of roles, not people. Standardizing can also remove real differences along with the bias (Van Vaerenbergh & Thomas 2013), so read this view beside the unadjusted one. To pool the boards, it treats each board's importance-type axis (critical, important, transformative, desirable) as one scale and its readiness-type axis (ready, soon, feasible, easy or cheap) as another; Q7's axes are swapped back for this.

Reading the twins. All nine twins share one scale. Each half-axis runs from the board's crossing (0) to its end label (±1), so every quadrant is the same size even where the hand-drawn axes weren't. The labels are as written on each board, and groups chose their own, so read each board against its own labels. Each shaded circle is centred on an average and holds the two-thirds of votes nearest it; votes outside it are the outliers it ignores. Role averages drawn hollow, with dashed circles, rest on three votes or fewer. Words in [brackets] are our additions, such as spelled-out acronyms.

Roles. Participants picked a sticker colour for their own role: research (light or dark blue), funding (green), risk (red or orange), policy (light or dark pink), advocacy (purple). White stickers were a backup for advocacy and don't show on white paper, so any white votes are missing here.

Groupings. The four headings on the overview are our reading of the votes, explained in the track's outputs summary.
