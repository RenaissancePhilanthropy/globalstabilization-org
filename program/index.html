Charlotte DeWald · Ginny Selz

To provide an overview of (1) Arctic climate risks and tipping points, how they are interconnected, and what we know about their risk timelines; and (2) the state of interventions research, covering the breadth of Arctic-targeted interventions and their respective timelines. With this established, the session will break out into three groups with facilitators exploring three central questions pertaining to the session theme. Then session participants will reconvene at the end to present synthesized findings and then discussion in plenary.

Melissa Barnes · Marianne Hagen · Charlotte Love · Brent Minchew · Ted Parson

This session examines the current state of scientific understanding on catastrophic sea-level rise, with particular attention to uncertainty surrounding Thwaites Glacier and its implications for forecasting, coastal planning, and governance. Speakers will address observed trends and outstanding research questions, requirements for decision-ready forecasting systems, standards for safely evaluating stabilization interventions, and governance frameworks appropriate to both research and potential future implementation. Breakout groups will turn discussion into concrete priorities across forecasting, adaptation, intervention research standards, and governance.

Lisa Dilling · Peter Frumhoff · Catherine Nakalembe · Danielle Potocek · Regina R. Rodrigues · Bridget Shayka · Julián Velasco

Living systems are already showing signs of changing state. Coral reefs, for example, are crossing thermal thresholds, boreal wildfires are accelerating, thawing the permafrost beneath them, and tropical wetlands are emitting more methane as they warm. These shifts ripple through climate, food, water, and coastal security.

This track opens with short scientific briefings, then works as a group to generate a ledger of open questions: what leading indicators of biosphere tipping might look like, where the science is thin, and which answers would change what happens next. A fishbowl discussion examines the biospheric risks of proposed interventions, including their effects on biodiversity. Output: a prioritized set of questions carried into Day 2 and the summit report.

Colleen Golja · Dakota Gruener · Megan Melamed · Pornampai (Ping-Ping) Narenpitak · Jesse Reynolds

This interactive session will explore the conditions needed to support a responsible path for SRM research over the next decade. Participants will consider how scientific knowledge, technology, institutions, and politics must co-develop to advance the field and support informed decision-making. Using Reflective's SAI Research Roadmap as one starting point, the session will test its assumptions, identify gaps, and consider what the roadmaps look like for other dimensions of this space, including marine cloud brightening and non-sulfur approaches. Small-group scenario discussions will examine how priorities may shift across plausible alternative future scenarios.

Theo Cox · Laurie Laybourn · Janani Vivekananda

Before we can act effectively in response to catastrophic climate risks, we must first make sense of them. This requires translating noisy signals into decision-relevant understanding, and ensuring this understanding is structured and located in a way that guides action. The world is a long way from having decision-support tools for catastrophic climate risk. This session will use a scenario exercise to explore the different sensemaking challenges posed by catastrophic climate risks: using the knowledge we have, generating and integrating the knowledge we don't, and navigating the uncertainty we can never overcome.

Shaun Fitzgerald · Elena Kavanagh · Ken Kitatani · Ernest Ofori · Sandy Trust

This session examines how social license is shaped by norms, operationalized by organizations, and maintained or terminated at the community level and beyond. The panel will explore how Earth systems stabilization institutions foster legitimacy and build public trust, drawing on lessons and best practices from other scientific fields.

Joshua Elliott · Kelly Erhart · George Horner · Andrew Light · Liam St Louis · Qi Zheng

What will it take to fund Earth systems stabilization at the scale the problem demands? This session looks at two linked stages: bringing new philanthropic capital into the field, and building toward sustained public research funding. Speakers will share what they’ve seen work, where the gaps are, and how today’s funding choices shape the field’s long-term credibility. Discussion follows.

Moderator: Brandon Jones · Steven Hamburg · Megan Melamed · Kate Ricke · Regina R. Rodrigues · others to be confirmed

This session asks why scientists choose to work on climate intervention and stabilization research — and what makes this young, often controversial field harder than it should be to join and stay in. We'll name the real costs directly (career risk, publication bias, public disputes) and use personal experience as evidence of the structural dynamics shaping the field's long-term credibility and survival.

13:00Block 2Five parallel sessions

Marian D’Auria · Anthony Hobley · Sandy Trust · Gernot Wagner

Financial returns and stability ultimately rest on a stable Earth system — yet this dependency remains largely unrecognised within financial services. This 90-minute session confronts that blind spot head-on. Following a short Planetary Solvency introduction, a live "risk management fail" reveal exposes the scale of climate risk underestimation among economic actors. A candid interview with senior financial services leaders then unpacks the real barriers to action: short-termism, unrealistic risk views, cognitive dissonance, reductionist thinking, low biosphere literacy, and genuine constraints on agency. Breakout groups — policymakers, financial services, and customers — then wrestle with who's actually responsible for change, before playback captures the session's collective insights.

Moderator: Oliver Morton · Facilitators: Ben Lachman & Paul Gambill · María Inés Carabajal · Julia Herrle · Ben Kalina · Mark Lynas · Cynthia Scharf · Mark Turner

How do we communicate about planetary risk and intervention research to audiences that need a completely different language: policymakers, scientists, funders, journalists, and the public? Four practitioners will present eight-minute case studies from four different communication frontlines, from countering misinformation on Capitol Hill to reaching the public through film.

Laurie Laybourn · Theo Cox

The stabilisation discourse is primarily focused on intervening to manage catastrophic biophysical risks in the Earth system, like AMOC collapse and rapid sea level rise. These risks would have catastrophic downstream impacts on societies. Yet severe, if not catastrophic, socioeconomic risks are possible even at relatively less severe states of Earth system change. What would a sibling category of stabilisation look like directly focused on catastrophic socioeconomic risks?

Richie Kaur · Ryan O’Donnell · Jessica Seddon · Shuchi Talati · Billy Williams

Climate change requires immediate action. Our most urgent, non-negotiable priority must be to tackle the root cause but as pressure grows to supplement emissions reductions with a variety of possible direct interventions in Earth’s climate systems, how can we ensure that research is done in ways that are ethical, inclusive, and representative? Join this session to explore the ethics of climate stabilization measures. From responsible research and transparency frameworks and tools; grassroots to policy makers, advocacy to industry, how can we center ethics in this fast-moving space?

Charlotte DeWald · Marianne Hagen · David Keith · Lauren Mahle · Yanai Yedvab · Fonger Ypma

Earth systems stabilization interventions show theoretical promise, but bridging the gap between modeling and remote, large-scale deployment remains a major obstacle. This session explores the practical realities of moving stabilizing interventions into the field at scale. Panelists will analyze the near-term technical readiness, infrastructure constraints, and operational challenges of scaling key approaches, including Stratospheric Aerosol Injection (SAI), Marine Cloud Brightening (MCB), Mixed-phase Cloud Thinning (MCT), and glacier and sea-ice interventions. Participants will synthesize lessons from the panelists via an interactive scenario planning exercise.
